## Developer interview protocol

Purpose: learn whether repeatable slowdown evidence is useful in a real debugging workflow. This is preparation for interviews, not evidence that interviews occurred. Start with about five consenting developers who use local GPU workloads; record actual completion and selection bias. Do not collect employer secrets, prompts, proprietary logs or personal identifiers unnecessarily. Ask before recording; otherwise keep deidentified notes. Do not upload a participant's trace without explicit permission for its destination and contents.

1. Describe the last GPU-memory performance problem you investigated. What was the application, environment and symptom?
2. What evidence distinguished it from compute load, intentional offload or host-memory pressure?
3. How often has this happened in your own work, and how much time did the last incident take? Record unknown values as unknown.
4. Which tools or traces did you use? What did they fail to explain?
5. Could you reproduce the same fixed task with a baseline and a recovery run? What prevented it?
6. What settings or task quality must a remedy preserve?
7. Show a labeled local report. Ask the participant to explain what it proves and does not prove without coaching.
8. Ask them to locate the primary metric, invalid outcomes, source hashes and limitations. Record completion time and errors rather than saying the tool is easy to use.
9. Would a reduced recipe change their next debugging action? Request a concrete example and compare with their current workflow.
10. What installation, privacy, performance or integration limits would prevent a trial? Would they independently run a bounded local study? Do not equate interest with adoption or payment.
